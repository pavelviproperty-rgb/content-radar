# Three fastest plausible paths to $1M founder liquidity

**Status:** hypothesis map, not an offer, valuation, tax or legal advice (2026-08-09).  
**Hard truth:** no researched evidence supports a high probability that a concept-stage snack brand will pay its founder $1M within 12 months. The fastest routes are strategic exceptions. Build option value while spending only against proof.

## First define the finish line

| Cash flow | Goes to | Counts toward founder's $1M? |
|---|---|---:|
| Primary investment / SAFE | Company | **No** |
| Founder secondary | Founder | **Yes**, gross |
| Cash acquisition consideration | Shareholders/assets seller | **Yes**, founder's allocated cash |
| Licensing royalty/advance | Usually company (unless founder owns/licences IP, which creates diligence/tax issues) | Only when lawfully distributed/paid to founder |
| Salary/consulting | Founder | Yes as cash, but ordinary compensation—not liquidity from equity—and may bind founder to remain |
| Earn-out | Founder/company later if conditions met | **Not at close; risk-adjust heavily** |
| Rollover equity | Founder | **No current liquidity** |

### Proceeds math

`Founder gross at close = (cash equity value − debt − preference payouts − transaction costs) × founder fully diluted ownership + direct founder payments at close.`

`After-tax cash = founder gross − federal/state/local tax − professional fees.`

**ESTIMATE:** To **net** about $1M, target **$1.45M–$1.80M gross cash to founder**, not a $1M headline. Illustrative haircut: 5–10% transaction/professional costs and 24–38% combined tax. Actual asset-versus-stock allocation, holding period, QSBS eligibility, state residence, earn-outs and ordinary-income components can change this materially. The IRS states net capital gains may face rates up to 20%; the 3.8% Net Investment Income Tax may also apply above thresholds. [IRS Topic No. 409, Capital Gains and Losses](https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc409); [IRS Questions and Answers on NIIT](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/questions-and-answers-on-the-net-investment-income-tax) (accessed 2026-08-09). Obtain transaction tax counsel before negotiating structure.

## Path 1 — Sell the transferable brand/IP/manufacturing package

**Target:** $1.5M–$2.5M mostly-cash asset or stock acquisition in **6–18 months**; founder owns 90–100%; limited escrow; no long earn-out.

**Minimum credible package:** standout blind-taste result; owned/assignable mark and domains; commercial formula/SOP with clear chain of title; repeatable pilot; credible shelf-life/safety file; at least two production quotes; compliant label; channel model; 300–1,000 non-friend purchasers or unusually strong preorder/retailer evidence; clean cap table/data room.

**Likely counterparty:** protein ingredient supplier, sports-nutrition company, confectionery platform or capable co-manufacturer for whom the format fills a documented portfolio/process gap.

**Trigger / action:** Run targeted non-confidential buyer discovery from day 30. If a credible buyer offers cash producing ≥$1M founder net with limited contingencies, **SELL**. Below that, seek a paid option/exclusivity only if non-refundable and short.

**Economics example (illustrative):** $2.0M debt-free cash equity value − $140k deal costs = $1.86M distributable; at 90% ownership founder gross = **$1.674M**. A 30% tax haircut leaves **$1.172M**. This is arithmetic, **not a valuation claim**.

**Probability:** **UNKNOWN; estimated low single digits inside 12 months**, perhaps 5–12% by 24 months conditional on an exceptional commercial product and genuine demand. Biggest failure: the “IP” is easy to copy and early sales are too small to justify buy-versus-build.

## Path 2 — Strategic investment with an explicit founder secondary and call/option

**Target:** strategic invests $1M–$3M primary and buys **$1.45M–$1.8M founder shares** at close in **9–24 months**, with a later acquisition option based on milestones.

**Minimum credible package:** validated commercial product, clean IP, buyer-specific fit, pilot economics, and stronger evidence than Path 1—typically a repeatable run plus retailer/distributor signal or $250k–$1M annualized net sales trajectory. Founder secondary must be a separate line item in documents.

**Structure guardrails:** independent board/shareholder approvals; same or transparently justified price; adequate company runway after secondary; no disguised redemption; limit repurchase clawbacks; define acquisition option price/floor and milestone measurement; avoid perpetual exclusivity.

**Trigger / action:** **RAISE + SECONDARY** only if founder cash is real at close and company capital is sufficient. A $3M “investment” with zero secondary does not meet the objective.

**Economics example:** strategic pays $1.5M directly for founder shares plus $1.5M primary. Founder gross = **$1.5M**; company cash = **$1.5M**; total financing headline = $3M. They are not interchangeable. At a 30% illustrative founder tax haircut, net = **$1.05M**.

**Probability:** **UNKNOWN; estimated 3–10% by 24 months conditional on traction.** Early investors often resist large founder secondaries because they want capital to fund growth. The strategic must value exclusivity/optionality enough to depart from that norm.

## Path 3 — Exclusive category/geography licence with upfront consideration, then distribute cash lawfully

**Target:** established producer/licensee pays company **$1.5M–$2.5M non-refundable upfront**, development/milestone fees and royalties in **6–18 months**, or directly acquires IP; founder receives ≥$1M only through a lawful dividend/distribution, IP sale, or contemporaneous founder-share purchase.

**Minimum credible package:** demonstrably differentiated know-how; documented process window; owned trademark/formula rights; licensee validation run; defined territory/category; credible economics; quality-control and audit provisions.

**Structure guardrails:** minimum guarantees; performance/launch deadlines; rights revert on nonperformance; licensee pays commercialization; limited exclusivity; improvements ownership defined; no transfer of the whole opportunity for a token advance. Board, solvency, tax and investor-consent constraints govern distributions.

**Trigger / action:** **LICENSE** if risk-adjusted guaranteed cash exceeds the expected value of self-launch and preserves unused categories/geographies. Prefer a licence plus purchase option with a meaningful floor over royalty-only promises.

**Economics example:** $2.0M upfront to company is still **$0 automatically to founder**. If company retains $400k for obligations and lawfully distributes $1.6M to a sole founder, the founder has $1.6M gross; tax character depends on structure. If investors/preferences exist, this shortcut may be impossible.

**Probability:** **UNKNOWN; estimated 2–8% by 24 months.** Biggest failures are no protectable/secret know-how, licensee unwillingness to pay a large advance before sales, and leakage of value through exclusivity.

## Ten deal-killing caveats

1. No buyer pays for founder kitchen know-how that is not documented and assignable.
2. Trademark conflict or descriptive weakness can make the brand package untransferable.
3. Formula/process may be easy to reverse engineer, eliminating licence leverage.
4. “High protein + low calorie” may taste materially worse than substitutes.
5. Humidity and moisture migration can destroy crispness before stated shelf life.
6. Low-density product can incur unacceptable fulfillment cost and shipping breakage.
7. A co-manufacturer may require an MOQ that consumes all working capital.
8. Retailer/distributor/broker margins can erase the attractive DTC spreadsheet margin.
9. Early revenue without cohort repeat is not durable demand and may not interest buyers.
10. Taxes, preference waterfalls, escrow, earn-outs and rollover can reduce headline value below the founder's cash objective.
