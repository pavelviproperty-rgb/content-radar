## Method

## Decision

1. **BEST FIRST PRODUCT — crisp meringue-style bites/cookies.** **INFERENCE:** an existing meringue line can plausibly make a small, intelligible product with less custom tooling than a rectangular bar. The format exposes the two existential risks quickly: taste at useful protein density and humidity/breakage. Use a multi-piece pouch or tray rather than promising a 20–30 g single serving immediately.
2. **BEST SECOND PRODUCT — filled protein wafer.** Existing wafer/bar infrastructure and excellent shipping/shelf-life make it commercially attractive, but it is less ownable as the category’s opening statement.
3. **BEST PRODUCT FOR EXIT STORY — WISP aerated crispy bar.** It communicates a proprietary texture/platform and conventional bar merchandising. Develop in parallel, but do not make commercial launch wait for bar geometry or 25 g protein.

## Kill gates before choosing a factory

- **Taste:** ≥60% preference against a leading protein-bar control in a blinded 50-person test; no more than 20% cite egg/whey aftertaste.
- **Texture:** water activity target and packaging specification set by a qualified food scientist; retain crispness through an accelerated study and a real-time checkpoint.
- **Shipping:** ≤3% visibly broken sale units after parcel simulation; otherwise redesign size/tray before paid acquisition.
- **Economics:** credible quote supports ≥40% fully loaded wholesale gross margin at the first repeatable MOQ (freight, duty and breakage included).
- **Demand:** ≥5% qualified landing-page preorder conversion at tested price, with refunds clearly available and product disclosed as prelaunch.

## Evidence anchors

The market already teaches consumers several adjacent formats: BUILT sells protein “Puffs,” Power Crunch sells wafer protein bars, Quest sells cheese-style protein crisps, and Halo Top sells light frozen desserts. These validate consumer comprehension—not WEIGHTLESS differentiation or demand.
